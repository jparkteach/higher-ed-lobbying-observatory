INTERPRET WITH CARE

A replication with a clear boundary.

What we can update

Institutional profiles, annual participation and spending, funding dependence, and Camp’s logistic and OLS model families. Systems are recombined before analysis to avoid counting allocated campus amounts as independent lobbying decisions.

What the file cannot answer

Media pressure, lobbying tactics, coalition motives, corporate comparisons, and causal policy effects. Camp’s interview and news evidence cannot be recreated from financial records. The field “direct” describes data assignment, not a coded lobbying tactic.

How the analysis is built

One row per system or standalone in the main models. Lobbying is “ever” during observed years; spending is the average per observed year. Funding dependence is a ratio of summed inflation-adjusted funds to summed valid budgets. Full definitions, exclusions and sensitivity results are in the report.

Sources, cleaning, and uncertainty

Camp, Matthew J. (2021), No Longer Exempt: Higher Education’s Entrée into Lobbying, Columbia University. Research questions: printed pp. 62–71; aggregation and variables: pp. 72–82; models: pp. 104–115; interviews and news: pp. 122–127.

Dataset: DISAGG_PANEL_2015_2024 2026.10.01.csv, supplied by the user. Its preselection criteria, funding-year alignment, dollar basis, and matches to lobbying filings were not independently audited. Dollar values are treated as nominal and converted using BLS annual CPI-U to 2022 dollars; verify the source’s dollar basis before scholarly publication.

Blank spending is zero only for explicit lobbied=0 records. PENDING stays missing. Nonpositive budgets do not produce funding ratios. Blank percentage fields are recomputed from supplied dollars. System finances sum the available member-campus fields; shared or missing finances can distort denominators. Main model confidence intervals use HC0 (logit) and HC3 (OLS); annual models include year indicators and cluster by entity. These are associations, not causal effects.
